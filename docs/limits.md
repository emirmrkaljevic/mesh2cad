# Limits (v0.27.0)

This is what the STEP fitter rebuilds, what becomes a named parameter in a script, and what is refused. Version numbers in the text are the releases where a measurement was taken. They are not a claim that older behavior is still current, except where the sentence says the output is unchanged.

Planes, cylinders, spheres, cones, constant-radius fillets between two planes, and quarter tori between a plane and a coaxial cylinder. Cylinders must end on planes perpendicular to their axis (through-holes, bosses, pins). Spheres are exact when each boundary loop is a single plane ∩ sphere circle (domes, spherical bosses, spherical pockets, and zones between two parallel planes), and when a box corner is a sphere octant bounded by three fillet cylinders, as below. A sphere that runs into an edge or a cylinder in any other way falls back to the hybrid solid; vertices are still snapped onto the fitted sphere, but the face stays faceted. Cones are exact when they run between two planes perpendicular to their axis: countersinks, chamfered holes and bosses, drafted/tapered bosses. Cones may also meet a coaxial cylinder (countersunk hole, chamfered pin). A cone that ends in its apex falls back to the hybrid solid. Countersinks and narrow chamfers are exact through about 0.05 mm of noise; a 2 mm chamfer and a wide drafted wall stay exact at 0.2 mm. The countersunk plate's 90° countersink (Ø6 hole, Ø12 head, 3 mm deep) at 0.2 mm noise is exact on one of three seeds. On the other two the conical band and the hole wall meet at a single vertex, so those seeds fall back to a valid faceted solid.

A fillet between two planes is a partial cylinder. When the round runs from one cap to another (rounded vertical corners of a plate or block, a single edge, the inner corner of an L as well as the outer ones), it comes back as one cylinder, tangent to both walls, with the radius in the report's cylinder faces. The radius is the one whose axis is tangent to both walls and which fits the arc; wall triangles that remain in the patch do not pull it off. On the synthetic plate (r=5 mm), block (r=2 mm) and L-bracket (r=3 mm) that rebuild is exact at 0 and 0.05 mm of noise, all three seeds. With no noise the radius error is 0.001 mm on the plate, 0.003 mm on the block and 0.005 mm on the bracket. At 0.05 mm the worst radius error is 0.041 mm on the r=2 corners, 0.100 mm on the r=5 plate and 0.131 mm on the r=3 bracket. At 0.2 mm the bracket falls back on every seed. The plate is exact on 2 of 3 seeds, and one of those solids is missing a corner. The block is a valid solid on every seed, but one seed keeps only three of the four rounds and the Hausdorff rises to 0.85 mm. Whatever does not sew stays a valid faceted solid.

On a clean tessellation (dihedral angles sitting on a few exact values, which is what a CAD export looks like), a region that crosses a real edge of about 35 degrees or more is split and each piece is refit. A flat polygon whose corners lie on a circle stays planes: the face centres are millimetres off that circle. A short 45 degree chamfer is kept when the residual is roundoff, which is how a countersink a few tenths of a millimetre tall survives. A ring of single-facet planes whose centres lie on one full cylinder is merged back into that cylinder, so a chamfer sees one wall instead of dozens of triangles. A hole end split across coplanar pads that do not share an edge is sewn as arcs of one circle. A plane corner whose three normals are linearly dependent, or whose intersection lands far from the mesh, uses the mesh vertex when it lies on the three planes. None of this runs on a noisy mesh. A fillet between two planes is not split.

A box with every edge filleted at one radius is rebuilt as 6 planes, 12 partial cylinders and 8 sphere octants. Each cylinder is tangent to the two walls it joins, each sphere is tangent to the three faces at that corner, and the fillet axis passes through the two corner centres, so the shared edges are the straight generators and the quarter circles. On the synthetic 40×30×20 mm box with r=3 mm this is exact on 3 of 3 seeds at 0, 0.05 and 0.2 mm noise. With no noise the radius error is 0.006 mm and the Hausdorff distance is 0.023 mm (volume error 0.01%). At 0.05 mm the worst radius error is 0.007 mm and the Hausdorff is 0.035 mm (volume error 0.07%). At 0.2 mm the worst radius error is 0.112 mm and the Hausdorff is 0.116 mm (volume error 0.18%). A fillet that meets a sphere in any other layout is not rebuilt and still falls back to a valid solid. A fillet between a plane and a coaxial cylinder is a quarter torus, tangent to both, and is rebuilt when the band closes around the axis. The report lists a torus face and its major and minor radius. Two shapes use the inner equator (major = cylinder radius + minor): the concave fillet at the base of a boss, and the convex round on the rim of a hole. The parametric scripts are one Z-up box, pads on its top face, and holes and straight cylindrical bosses whose axis is X, Y, or Z. All twelve edges of a rectangular base, when they share one radius, are `edge_fillet_r`. A through-hole whose wall clears those rounds is cut after the fillet. A straight boss on the top face whose wall clears those rounds is fused after the fillet. A through hole runs from one face of the base to the opposite face. A blind hole opens on one face; `hole_N_depth` is the distance from that face to the flat end. A side boss sits on one side face and grows straight out. Countersinks, drafts, and chamfers are still vertical only. A quarter-torus fillet is a parameter only when its axis is Z: `boss_N_fillet` on a straight boss, `hole_N_rim_r` and `hole_N_bot_rim_r` on a through hole, and `hole_N_floor_r` on a blind floor. The same fillet on X or Y is refused. An axis that is not aligned with X, Y, or Z is refused, and so is a side feature that does not open on a face of the base.

Plain Z through-holes of one diameter are grouped when they form a row of three or more, an axis-aligned grid of at least two by two, or a bolt circle of three or more. The row is `hole_row_N_n`, `hole_row_N_pitch`, `hole_row_N_d`, the start `hole_row_N_x` / `hole_row_N_y`, and `hole_row_N_angle` in degrees. The grid is `hole_grid_N_nx`, `hole_grid_N_ny`, `hole_grid_N_pitch_x`, `hole_grid_N_pitch_y`, and the same kind of corner and angle. The bolt circle is `hole_circle_N_n`, `hole_circle_N_d`, `hole_circle_N_r` (pitch circle radius, center to hole center), `hole_circle_N_cx`, `hole_circle_N_cy`, and `hole_circle_N_angle` (degrees, 0 along +X, folded into -180/n to 180/n). Foot and upright holes on an L-bracket use the `base_hole_` and `leg_hole_` prefixes, including `base_hole_circle_*` and `leg_hole_circle_*`. A fully filleted box uses `hole_circle_*`, cut after `edge_fillet_r`. Two holes are not a row and not a circle. A set that is also a row or an axis-aligned grid keeps that pattern. One missing hole on an otherwise regular circle stays `hole_N_*`. A blind hole, a countersink, a counterbore, and a hole along X or Y are not grouped. Centers have to lie within max(4·tol, 0.25) mm of the ideal position. A patterned script that misses the deviation gate is replaced by the individual `hole_N_*` parameters. Extrusions and combined booleans are not grouped.

A straight obround through-slot along Z on a plate is `slot_N_len` (center to center), `slot_N_w` (width, the end diameter), the center `slot_N_x` / `slot_N_y`, and `slot_N_angle`. Angle 0 is along +X and 90 is along +Y. A measured angle other than those two is kept when both round ends and both straight walls agree. On the 60×40×6 mm plates in the [CadQuery notes](cadquery.md), one axis-aligned slot is written at 0, 0.05, and 0.2 mm noise, two slots of different orientation are written at 0 and 0.05 mm and refused at 0.2 mm, and a 30 degree slot is written at 0 and 0.05 mm, refused on 1 of 3 seeds at 0.2 mm. A rectangular opening stays `cutout_N`. A round hole stays `hole_N_*`. A blind stadium and a slot whose ends have different diameters are refused by the same deviation gate, and they are not rewritten as `slot_N_*`.

A straight extrusion of one outline is written as well, along X, Y, or Z. The outline may be an L, a notch, or a slanted edge. `extrude_len` is the length, `extrude_0` is where it starts, and `outline` is the point list (YZ when the extrusion is along X, XZ along Y, XY along Z). A polygonal opening through that extrusion is `cutout_N`. A round through-hole keeps `hole_N_d` and its position. A counterbored through-hole adds `hole_N_cbore_d` and `hole_N_cbore_depth`. When the box script already matches the mesh it is the one that is written, so a plate, a pad, and a side hole stay on `base_x`, `hole_1_d`, and `fillet_r`.

A part made of a few of those extrusions is written too, when every region is a plane, an axis-aligned cylinder, or a 45 degree cone on a principal axis. The solid is the intersection of the silhouettes along X, Y, and Z, or one of those extrusions minus straight cut extrusions along the other axes. A single outline keeps the names above. Two or three use `extrude_z_len` / `outline_z` and the same for X and Y. A cut is `cut_1`, `cut_1_len`, and `cut_1_0`. A cut may be blind, with one open end. A shallow floor parallel to an exterior cap, such as a hex pocket or a logo, is the same kind of cut; a non-circular island inside it is `cut_N_cutout_J`. A face tilted to all three axes, at most four of them, is a planar clip: `clip_1_x`, `clip_1_y`, `clip_1_z`, `clip_1_d` with the outward normal and `n·x = d`. Intersected extrusions overlap by 0.02 mm in the script so the shared end faces stay a manifold; the other outline stops at the true end, so the nominal solid does not grow. Cuts are grown by the same 0.02 mm, so a pocket that opens on an outer face is not left with that skin. If the fused silhouette is more than 3% smaller than a filled raster of the mesh, the raster outline replaces it. This replacement runs only after the analytic solid has already missed the gate, so a part whose fused outline is already right does not change. endstop-block is this case (three silhouettes, one counterbored hole, one clip), and both scripts deviate from the mesh by 0.0421 mm. y-belt-idler is the same vocabulary with 45 degree hole chamfers, a blind hole, and the floor cuts, and both scripts deviate by at most 0.1639 mm.

A round hole on a combined extrusion may be a through hole, a counterbored through hole, or a blind hole (`hole_N_depth`, measured from the face it opens on). A counterbore whose wall is only part of a turn, because the bore breaks out of a side face, is recovered from that ring of planes when the facets sit on one principal cylinder, the arc is one span, and the bore inside the arc is empty. A tangent fillet, and a flat polygon whose corners merely lie on a circle, are not read as a hole wall. When the mouth stops short of the bounding end and the gap is already empty, the counterbore depth is measured from that end. This fit is not applied during segmentation, so a STEP file does not gain the cylinder. A 45 degree cone on that hole is `hole_N_chamfer`, the axial length of the cone, either a mouth chamfer or a taper at the inner end of a blind hole. A draft and a countersink that is not 45 degrees are still refused. A single extruded outline still has no chamfer: those stay vertical, and only on the box vocabulary.

When that intersection misses the mesh, the same gate is applied to a union of partial-depth extrusions. Two outlines on one axis are `extrude_z1_len` / `outline_z1` and `extrude_z2_len` / `outline_z2` (and the same for X and Y). Each keeps its own start. A joint overlaps by 0.02 mm; the named length stays the true length. A clip that belongs to one extrusion is `extrude_z2_clip1_x` and the same for y, z, and d, and it cuts only that extrusion. A 45 degree chamfer on the start face of a Z extrusion is `extrude_z1_chamfer` (the leg) and `extrude_z1_chamfer_at` (the outline edges that carry it). A hole that opens on an intermediate face carries `hole_N_z0` and `hole_N_z1` (or x or y), and the counterbore is measured from that span, not from the far end of a taller neighbour. y-motor-holder is this case. Both scripts deviate by 0.1843 mm (CadQuery volume error 0.306%, OpenSCAD 0.298%), under the limit of 0.266 mm. On 0.12.0 the same part was refused at 7.924 mm and 30.07% volume, which was the visual hull of the full-length silhouettes.

When the partial-depth union also misses the gate, the outlines are rebuilt from a raster of the mesh with each edge snapped to a vertical wall. A gap that follows a hole cylinder is closed by the chord of that hole, unless the mesh sits outside the cylinder, in which case those points stay. A planar bevel whose two joining walls are axis-aligned is a triangular cut (`cut_N`) along the edge, over the span where the face is a strip rather than a single tip. A chord of a known hole is not treated as that bevel. A 45 degree cone that was not used as `hole_N_chamfer` is its own cutter: `cone_N_r0`, `cone_N_r1`, `cone_N_h`, at `cone_N_x`, `cone_N_y`, `cone_N_z`. Shallow floors are tried again on this solid and kept only when the solid moves closer to the mesh. None of this runs when an earlier solid already passed the gate. y-rod-holder is this case. Both scripts deviate by 0.1300 mm (CadQuery volume error 0.096%, OpenSCAD 0.089%), under the limit of 0.138 mm.

When the wall-snapped rebuild also misses the gate, the part is cut back from one bounding extrusion along the axis whose section area moves the most. Stations are taken along that axis. A run of stations whose outer profile stays within 0.05 mm is one cut: the bounding rectangle, with the section kept as `cut_N_cutout_J`. A gap between two parallel faces thinner than those stations (down to 0.12 mm) is a polygonal cut of the section between the faces. An inner loop that keeps its centre and its area from one station to the next, such as a hex pocket or a bore, is a polygonal cut over that span. This does not run when an earlier solid already passed the gate. The gate is unchanged.

Still refused, and the command says which: a boss on an extruded outline; a countersink, chamfer, or draft on a single extruded outline; a bevel whose walls are not axis-aligned, or whose section is not the right triangle of the face; a rounded edge that is not the four vertical corners of a rectangular base, not all twelve edges of that base at one radius, not the vertical edges of a filleted L-bracket, and not a vertical boss-base fillet, hole-rim round, or blind-floor fillet; a hole on that L-bracket that is not a Z through-hole sitting in the foot or the upright; a torus fillet whose axis is not Z; a stepped hole that is not a counterbored through-hole; more than four faces tilted to X, Y, and Z (that check runs before the partial-depth union); a recess that is not a straight cut, a shallow floor, or a section cut of the kind above; a sphere other than the eight corners of a fully filleted box (those corners stay part of `edge_fillet_r` when a through-hole is cut through the box, or a boss is fused onto it); an unfitted region. A flat patch that the fitter returned as a sphere is written as the plane its vertices lie on, so a noisy counterbore floor is not refused as a sphere. Free-form surfaces stay on the STEP path.

On the 60×40×36 block (Ø8 through hole along X at y=0, z=−6; Ø6 blind hole, 12 deep, opening on +Y at x=−12, z=8; Ø10 boss, 8 tall, on +X at y=10, z=8) both scripts recover those dimensions with no noise and at 0.05 mm noise. With no noise the CadQuery script matches the mesh to 0.006 mm (volume error 0.004%) and the OpenSCAD script to 0.006 mm (volume error 0.003%). At 0.05 mm noise the check against that noisy mesh is 0.139 mm / 0.015% for CadQuery and 0.149 mm / 0.016% for OpenSCAD, under the same limit as every other script, max(4·tol, 0.1) = 0.20 mm. The blind end is not its own plane on this part (the disk stays in the cylinder region), so the depth is the median of the vertices clustered at that end. The exact STEP builder does not sew that cylinder; STEP export of this block falls back to a valid faceted solid, Hausdorff 0.004 mm from the mesh. Anything else outside this vocabulary stays on the STEP path.

The FreeCAD macro is that same vocabulary and the same refusal list. `fillet_r` is the four vertical corners of a rectangular base, and also every vertical edge of an L-bracket, including the inner corner. `edge_fillet_r` fillets every edge of the box. A through-hole that clears those rounds is cut after `makeFillet`, and a boss that clears them is fused after it. `makeFillet` also rounds the circular edge named by `boss_N_fillet`, `hole_N_rim_r`, `hole_N_bot_rim_r`, or `hole_N_floor_r`. A boss on an extruded outline, a countersink that is not 45 degrees on a combined extrusion, a sphere, and an unfitted region are still refused, and the message says which. The macro's cylinders and cones are analytic. The check meshes the solid at 0.01 mm linear deflection, which on these parts is a few thousandths of a millimetre, the same kind of gap as OpenSCAD's `$fn = 96`. The file you edit is a Part script, not a PartDesign tree, so a dimension change is one number at the top and another run of the macro.
